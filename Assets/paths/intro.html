The Celebration of Romanticism

Poetry is a language.

It uses a combination of language techniques packaged in a small wordcount to convey complex ideas.

Romanticism uses poetry and other mediums to convey its own ideals. These ideals are ones of nature, imagination, childhood, emotions and the individual.

Many look at poetry, and in extension, Romanticism and don't care. It appears as a boring combination of words, like a worse version of a book. But once you decipher it, you find its true meaning and the beauty that arises from that.

Today, instead of reading slowly and analysing these words on paper out of our own choice, we are shoved with bright screens giving use useless, unwanted information constantly. Poetry allows one to slow down, concentrate and learn.

"[Poetry] allows one to concentrate. Eliminate chaos." Hiyoshi Springs Poet, Ghost of Tsushima

Romanticism deeply reflects poetry's idea of taking it slow, admiring what is around you and writing it done in a foreign language, the language of Poetry.

Society frequently chooses greed and sin over other ideas, and corruption comes out of the values society creates. Through the portrayal of innocence and experience of the children as chimney sweepers, the chimney sweeper illustrates the corruption of church in those times, and how these kids were brainwashed into this life. For instance, throughout the innocence version the idea of soot on them clinging to them is present throughout. This is a metaphor for societies sin as these kids get all of the greed and other sins dumped on them. The audience is invited to reflect how children like these were abused for other peoples sins and how everyone’s sins must go somewhere, not just left around. In the experience version, the author talks about “[society] are gone to praise God and his Priest and King”, after saying they had done no injury to the chimney sweepers. Blake uses an Anaphora to highlight how society values the “Priest” and “King” - despite the core beliefs of the church saying to value God. This shows how sin causes corruption even to powerful people or people in the church, causing people like the chimney sweeper to suffer. The experience version’s rhyming scheme starts with AABB and changes to ABAB later. This references the innocence version and combined with other similarities creates a transition or addressing the innocence version, showing how these kids started innocent and listening to societies values and brainwashed into these jobs. But the experience version addresses that and shows the audience how eventually after the work is done, they are nothing to society, they were lied to and are completely alone, like "a little black thing in the snow", something that doesn't fit and has no purpose. This ultimately illustrates the corruption of the church because of the greed in people like the Priest and King, and how they brainwash vulnerable people full of innocence, until they are experienced and left seemingly empty with "the clothes of death", or societies sin, wrapped around them.

historical context

Romanticism rose like a black swan in a time of industrialisation.

Romanticism began in the late 1700's as a push against the Age of Enlightenment. It is a literary movement that supports nature, sublime and indiviudality to name a few, which oppose the ideals that are presented in Enlightenment, Industrialisation and Scientific Revolution which where all big at the time. It opposed the "new" methods and support "old" methods a lot more.

The "Big Six" of Romanticism

Despite being born in different generations, there are 6 men that their work in Romanticism earned them the title of The Big Six. The group consists of William Blake, William Wordsworth, Samuel Taylor Coleridge, Lord Byron, Percy Bysshe Shelley and John Keats.

The Romanticism Ideals

Awe of Nature

Romanticism values the beauty and power of nature. At the time, nature was not cared for as innovation in science and industry was valued.

Strong Emotions

Romanticism likes to look at emotions on a deep level. In the context of it's time, commonly emotions were not valued as much, especially for another human's emotions.

Imagination

Romanticism values imagination as a tool that we can use. Imagination was valued in a different way at it's time, more focusing on innovation and pushing scientific breakthroughs, instead of Romanticism's slow, emotional pace.
